# DIALECTICA — Core Algorithm References

1. **Lindley, D. V. (1956).** On a Measure of the Information Provided by an Experiment. *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 27(4), 986–1005. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177728069. Foundation for information-based experimental utility.
2. **Atkinson, A. C., & Fedorov, V. V. (1975).** Optimal Design: Experiments for Discriminating between Several Models. *Biometrika*, 62(2), 289–303. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/62.2.289. Classical model-discrimination design; V0.7's scalar mean-separation diagnostic is not a full implementation of this method.
3. **Hainy, M., Price, D. J., Restif, O., & Drovandi, C. (2022).** Optimal Bayesian Design for Model Discrimination via Classification. *Statistics and Computing*, 32, 25. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-022-10078-2. Supports classifier-based approximate model discrimination when likelihood calculations are difficult.
4. **Huan, X., Jagalur, J., & Marzouk, Y. (2024).** Optimal Experimental Design: Formulations and Computations. *Acta Numerica*, 33, 715–840. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492924000023. Broad modern review of Bayesian, decision-theoretic, computational, and sequential optimal experimental design.
5. **Settles, B. (2009).** Active Learning Literature Survey. University of Wisconsin–Madison Computer Sciences Technical Report 1648. https://minds.wisc.edu/items/37538f44-36ae-413e-8967-e6c831e17a8e. Background for query selection and active learning.
6. **Wald, A. (1945).** Sequential Tests of Statistical Hypotheses. *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 16(2), 117–186. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118. Foundation for sequential probability ratio testing; not yet implemented in V0.7.
7. **Kuncheva, L. I., & Whitaker, C. J. (2003).** Measures of Diversity in Classifier Ensembles and Their Relationship with the Ensemble Accuracy. *Machine Learning*, 51, 181–207. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022859003006. Supports careful use of ensemble diversity and double-fault measures; it also cautions that diversity does not universally imply better accuracy.
