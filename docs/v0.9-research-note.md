# DIALECTICA V0.9 — R-IDeA Reproduction and Proxy Failure Analysis

## Executive result

A finite-grid reproduction of the paper's polynomial experiment now runs with 20 seeds and 10 total designs per run. It includes Random, BAD/EIG, R-I, R-IDeA, a current-posterior-initialized proxy ablation, and an R-IDeA oracle using the best-in-class L2 projection.

The strongest result is conditional, not universal:

- In the quadratic-DGP / linear-learner case, R-IDeA with the zero-initialized linear proxy substantially improves over R-I at lambda=0.5, tau=0.5.
- In the well-specified quadratic control, all methods are close, as expected.
- In the cubic-DGP / linear-learner case, the learned-proxy R-IDeA is only marginally better than R-I at lambda=0.5 and does not beat Random. The oracle is much better than the learned proxy, indicating that proxy quality is the main unresolved bottleneck.
- Hyperparameters matter sharply. With lambda=0.25, both R-I and R-IDeA collapse in the cubic case; with lambda=1, both become much more representative and perform far better. With tau=10, R-IDeA degrades severely, including in the well-specified control.

This is not a universal robustness guarantee. It is a controlled reproduction of a specific active experimental-design problem.

The exact conjugate Gaussian posterior and analytic EIG replace the paper implementation's stochastic Pyro/NUTS calculations. Test metrics include both function risk and expected noisy-observation MSE (function risk + 0.01).

## Hyperparameter sensitivity

All rows use 20 runs and 10 total designs, with proxy margin tau=2 unless explicitly marked as shared-tau.

| lambda | DeA tau | Quadratic misspecification R-IDeA | Cubic misspecification R-IDeA | Cubic Random | Cubic R-I |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.25 | 0.5 | 24.2384 | 41.6213 | 12.0638 | 41.5656 |
| 0.5 | 0.1 | 6.8457 | 24.4357 | 12.0638 | 26.2588 |
| 0.5 | 0.5 | 6.9141 | 25.7753 | 12.0638 | 26.2588 |
| 0.5 | 2 | 11.9805 | 29.2202 | 12.0638 | 26.2588 |
| 0.5 | 10 | 23.0626 | 36.6587 | 12.0638 | 26.2588 |
| 1.0 | 0.5 | 6.0521 | 10.3649 | 12.0638 | 10.0010 |

The lambda=0.25 cubic failure matches the paper's explicit warning that low representativeness weighting can make R-I and R-IDeA worse than Random. The tau=10 failure demonstrates why a large de-amplification threshold cannot be assumed safer. In this reproduction, tau=0.5 is much safer than tau=10, while tau choice still interacts with misspecification degree.

The literal shared-tau proxy-margin variant (proxy margin tau=0.5) gives nearly identical results to the supplementary-code setting (proxy margin tau=2) at lambda=0.5, DeA tau=0.5 in this particular benchmark. This does not establish equivalence generally.

## Important algorithmic finding: the proxy is the bottleneck

For the cubic DGP, the oracle using the correct best-in-class projection reaches MSE 13.3477, while the learned proxy reaches 25.7753. That gap is much larger than the R-IDeA vs R-I difference.

The next algorithmic objective should therefore be to improve the approximation of f_bar, not merely to add more debate agents or multiply acquisition scores. Candidate directions:

1. Proxy ensemble / multi-start optimization with diagnostics for objective value, candidate-space disagreement coverage, and stability.
2. Adaptive tau selection using a held-out validation distribution, not the final test set.
3. A model-class-aware proxy basis, with a strict separation between paper/code reproduction and any improved variant.
4. Joint lambda/tau selection under a fixed evaluation budget, including a reject/abstain condition when no candidate is robust across plausible settings.
5. A held-out family of DGPs to test whether improvements transfer beyond the quadratic/cubic toy problems.

## Next stage

V0.9.1 should implement a proxy-ensemble and adaptive-threshold ablation, then evaluate on new held-out seeds and a higher-order DGP. Only after the proxy closes part of the oracle gap should we attempt a transfer to DIALECTICA's debate engine, where the target distribution and prediction metric must be operationally defined.
